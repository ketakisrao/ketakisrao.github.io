## 4. Engineering Principles & Staff Philosophy

### Technology in Service of Humans
- Tech should support humans in what they need, not the other way around.
- System design starts from user needs and mental models, not internal system convenience.

### Numbers & People
- *"Numbers tell you what happened, user conversations tell you why."*
- Telemetry shows drop-offs and metrics, but user interviews reveal the underlying reason.
- Listens, absorbs, and reads between the lines in user interviews to uncover what users truly need.
- Solves problems rather than guessing, and always quantifies results with appropriate metrics.

### Long-Term Scalability & Extensibility
- Backends must support great UX and be extensible to accommodate new features without rework.
- Strong preference for long-term scalability and robust engineering over quick hacks.

### Engineer for Production (Production Readiness)
- A prototype that works in isolation is not finished software.
- Whether deploying distributed backend services or generative AI agents, engineers continuous evaluations, offline test suites, and grounding guardrails so systems perform reliably under real-world traffic.

### Why Breadth Matters
- Refuses to be siloed. Believes breadth makes an engineer sharper, expands leverage, and prevents architectural and organizational blind spots.
- Understanding the full spectrum—from human psychology and frontend UX down to distributed backends, AI models, and organizational dynamics—enables building systems that fit human and business realities.

### Influence Without Authority & Team Alignment
- When disparate teams solve the same problem in competing ways, or when requirements are vague:
  1. Pull telemetry and data to establish ground truth.
  2. Get engineers and stakeholders together to define a shared definition of success.
- Translates fuzzy product requirements into crisp technical direction.

### Definition of Impact
- Impact means real user adoption, engagement, and alignment (e.g. UX translating to higher NPS or revenue).
- Architecturally handling scale and solving distributed systems problems.
- Does not stop at launch: tracks features over the next 6 months to address drop-offs and latency bumps until reaching target adoption (e.g. 80%+ of the user base).
- Developer Experience (DX): Tracks impact across developers, learns emerging tools, and actively shares knowledge in guilds through demos and presentations. Cares about DX as much as UX.

## 5. Staff Unlearnings (Key Mental Shifts)
1. **Unlearn doing everything yourself**: Shift identity from generating code/boilerplate to reviewing and exercising judgment.
2. **Unlearn equating effort with value**: Focus on outcomes over hours. Measure impact post-launch and partner with data analysts to tell the story of impact.
3. **Unlearn waiting until you know how**: Navigate internal documentation and codebase tools to build context independently before asking for help.
4. **Unlearn protecting expertise by staying in your lane**: Breadth matters. Understanding the whole system (frontend, backend, UX research) makes an engineer sharper and shifts focus from building tasks to building value.
5. **Unlearn decomposing work by role / ticket**: Look beyond the ticket to company strategy (NPS, revenue, churn). Move from task contributor to outcome owner.
6. **The Staff Transition**: Less execution, more judgment. Less ownership of tasks, more ownership of outcomes. Less *"I built this"*, more *"this is why it mattered."*
